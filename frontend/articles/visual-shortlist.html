FIELD NOTE / 01 / INDIA · BENGALURU

Kannada signs, autorickshaws and road form become a reviewable shortlist.

A familiar Indian street can carry useful clues without a famous landmark. The task is to separate a regional hint from evidence for a particular road.

WhereWas field notes · Illustrative example · 3 min read

  • Readable shop text
  • Transport context
  • Road geometry

A shopfront, an autorickshaw and a road divider tell different parts of a story. This Bengaluru example shows why everyday Indian streets belong in visual location search: the useful evidence is often ordinary, distributed across the frame and easy to miss at first glance.

Read the sign before naming the city.

When Kannada lettering is clear enough to read, it can support a Karnataka hypothesis. It does not uniquely identify Bengaluru. A legible shop name or locality can narrow the search further, but a chain name may point to many branches. Blurred characters should stay uncertain rather than becoming invented words.

The photo here is a reference scene, not an OCR benchmark. Its smaller signs are not all readable at this display size. A review should preserve the original wording and make uncertain readings visible.

Use the rest of the street to test the text.

Autorickshaws add transport context, but appear in many Indian cities. Compare stable details instead: the position of a junction, the width of the divider, building setbacks and the order of neighboring storefronts. Cars and temporary signs can change between capture dates.

A familiar street should produce candidates you can examine, not confidence you have to take on trust.

Inspect a candidate before accepting it.

  1. Search readable names and regional clues together.
  2. Compare the candidate's road layout with the uploaded scene.
  3. Open available reference imagery and check several fixed features from a plausible viewing direction.

WhereWas brings the shortlist, supporting clues and map context into one workspace. A nearby panorama is a way to inspect a hypothesis; its presence alone does not prove that the upload depicts that spot. Keep a wider radius when the evidence only supports a district or city.

Read the evidence, not just the pin.

These field notes describe a review process, not a measured success rate. Nearby street imagery is not proof of a visual match. Candidate coordinates should be interpreted with their confidence, supporting evidence and radius.
