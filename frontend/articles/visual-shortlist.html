Field note / visual search

How a road, sign, and skyline become a location shortlist.

A useful visual search begins with what is actually visible—then turns separate clues into candidates a person can inspect.

Illustrative field note · 6 min read

One photograph can hold many place signals: road markings, a shop name, a transit vehicle, building materials, language, street furniture, and the shape of a skyline. Any one clue may be weak. Together, they begin to constrain the search.

Start with evidence, not a guess

The best first output is a shortlist. It gives each candidate enough context to be compared rather than presenting a single point as guaranteed fact.

A location result becomes useful when the person reviewing it can see why it belongs on the list.

Make the clues visible

Showing the strongest observed clues gives users a way to spot errors quickly. It also lets them add their own context before the search proceeds to map or ground-level verification.

WhereWas field notes are illustrative product content. A result should always reflect the strength—and the limits—of the visible evidence.
