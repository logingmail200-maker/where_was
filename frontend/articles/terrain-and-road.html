Field note / geography

When the landscape carries the clues.

A road without a readable place name can still suggest regions. Terrain, vegetation, design and markings must be weighed together.

Illustrative field note · 5 min read

Start with observable details. A road's lane markings, guardrails, signs and pavement are often more specific than a general impression of the sky. The surrounding trees, slope and ground cover help test whether a candidate region is plausible.

Geography narrows; it rarely proves

Similar vegetation and road shapes occur in many countries. A Swiss-looking road is a hypothesis, not a confirmed address. Search should return several candidate regions with an honest radius, then compare them against licensed ground imagery or another independent clue.

Strong location evidence comes from several clues that agree, not one landscape resemblance.

Show what changed the ranking

A result is easier to review when it names the observed terrain, road signs, barrier design and any text that influenced the shortlist. If those clues do not distinguish candidates, the radius should stay wide.
