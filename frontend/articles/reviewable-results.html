Why a reviewable shortlist is better than a magic pin.

A single answer can look decisive. A useful interface preserves the alternatives, supporting evidence, and uncertainty behind it.

Illustrative field note · 4 min read

A map pin is the end of a process, not the process itself. When a photograph contains partial or conflicting signals, presenting only one answer hides the uncertainty a user most needs to see.

Rank candidates, do not manufacture certainty

A strong result gives users the leading candidates, the clues supporting each one, and a way to compare them against a map or available ground imagery.

Trust grows when a system shows its strongest answer and the evidence that could change it.

Make correction part of the workflow

When users can mark a result correct, close, or wrong, the product becomes more useful over time while keeping the final decision in human hands.
